## Who we are {#essa}

The **Edmonton Speed Skating Association** (ESSA) is the only speed skating club in Alberta's capital region. Under the guidance of our parent organizations, [Speed Skating Canada](https://speedskating.ca/) and [Speed Skating Alberta](https://speedskatingalberta.ca/), we provide both short track and long track speed skating opportunities to members of all ages.

### Our mission

To promote, support and develop speed skating in the Greater Edmonton area, to enable skaters, coaches, officials, and volunteers of all ages and abilities to achieve their full potential.

### Our values

We aim to provide a fun and positive experience whether through achievement, affiliation, participation or leadership. Speed skating is part of a healthy lifestyle. It connects people, helping to strengthen families and build communities, and develop character through principles of fair play. We operate according to sound ethical standards and principles of social, economic, and environmental sustainability that allow us to maintain viable programs. The association provides opportunities for personal growth and development through sport.

### Our vision

ESSA will be strong and vibrant, providing opportunities for all to participate as athletes, coaches, volunteers, and/or officials. The Association will focus on short track and long track programming, and will collaborate with the inline skating and Special Olympian communities. Speed skating will be a sport of choice in the Edmonton region supported by the public and private sectors.

## Why speed skating? {#why}

**Speed skating provides unparalleled flexibility among ice-based sports.** Unlike team sports, or sports where you pay a coach directly for each session, speed skating lets families attend training when it works for them. If you can't make a particular training session, that's not a problem. Take that winter vacation; we'll be here when you get back.

**A fixed training schedule** means you know at registration when your training times will be. No guesswork when planning your family's other activities.

**Choose the level of competition that makes sense for your family.** Whether you like to travel the province, race locally, or not race at all, you're in charge of your competition schedule.

**Keep your family together.** Races have multiple ability and age categories, so your 7-year-old, 10-year-old and 13-year-old can all race at the same event. Training times are often back to back, which means one trip to the rink or oval for everyone.

**Get outside and enjoy winter.** We spend our fair share of time in the arena, but from December through February we often train outdoors at Canada's most scenic skating oval in Victoria Park.

**Experience a community like no other.** Almost every ESSA family joined without any prior experience in speed skating. We know what it's like to be new, and everyone is willing to help out and answer questions. Spend a little time with us and you'll soon understand why so many families stay with this wonderful sport.

## Our history {#history}

### Places

Speed skating has a long and distinguished history in Edmonton. Before 1948, speed skating was held by several community clubs at hockey rinks, with races at local winter carnivals. Speed skating finals were held at the Arena (Edmonton Gardens) or at the 119th Street rink north of Jasper Avenue, which was covered and had natural ice.

In 1947, after local athletes Doreen Ryan (Junior) and Don Wynn (Intermediate) won Canadian Championships in Sudbury, Ontario, the City of Edmonton said it would provide a speed skating track on the condition that all community clubs amalgamate into one Edmonton Speed Skating Association.

In 1948 a 200 m track was prepared in Rossdale's Diamond Park, and ESSA formed in 1949. This was soon changed to a 268 m track, and a warm-up building was built alongside the adjacent hockey rink. In 1959 the Canadian Olympic Trials were held on this track.

Edmontonian Doreen Ryan made the 1960 Olympic Team and saw her first 400 m track in Squaw Valley. This was the first Olympiad to include women's speed skating events. Ms. Ryan skated for Canada again at the 1964 Olympics in Innsbruck, Austria.

The need for a full 400 m oval prompted a move to the old soccer pitch at Clarke Stadium (Commonwealth Stadium), where a warm-up facility was provided. Around 1968, Edmonton's Paul Enoch set the 5000 m Canadian record with a time of 7:59 on this track. The record lasted 12 years. In 1969, after years of problems growing grass on the soccer field, the track was moved to Coronation Park.

Murray Gardiner was ESSA's head coach from 1962 to 1974. He was an early promoter of Olympic-style speed skating in Canada, and his leadership produced many Canadian champions and Olympians. Canadian champions included Bob Hodges, Frank Ludke, Paul Enoch, Andrew Barron, Tom Overend, Cam Tipping and Verna Overend. Olympians included Bob Hodges, Frank Ludke, Paul Enoch, Andrew Barron and Tom Overend. Gardiner was also coach of the 1972 Canadian Olympic Speed Skating Team.

From 1969 to 1975 the club practised at Coronation Park, where the City flooded the running track. However, the corners did not have the required 25 m radius, so the track couldn't be used for competition, and Hawrelak Park hosted the 1974 Alberta Championships instead.

In 1974, national team member Cam Tipping found the Coronation Park track inadequate for his training. Jack Tipping and Bud Hall of ESSA, working with the City of Edmonton, suggested Victoria Park as a possible competition track. In the fall of 1975 the City enlarged the small skating rink in Victoria Park to a 333 m speed skating oval. In December 1975 the Canadian National Team Trials were held at Victoria Park, with Canadian champions Cam Tipping (from ESSA) and Craig Webster taking part. Several Alberta Championships were held there between 1975 and 1985, the largest being the Open Alberta Championships on January 31 and February 1, 1981, where future Olympic champion Catriona Le May Doan set two Saskatchewan records as a 10-year-old. Larger competitions weren't possible because of a lack of dressing rooms and space for officials.

In 1985 the club was gearing up for the 1988 Olympics with several talented young hopefuls, such as Geoff Williams and Colin Davidson, and the 333 m oval was felt to be inadequate for that level of training. A Community Recreation Grant from Edmonton Parks and Recreation paid to enlarge the oval to a 400 m Olympic-size track. Three ESSA members went on to be forerunners at the 1988 Olympics, and ESSA member Arty Lancaster represented Canada at the 1990 Junior World Championships as Canadian champion. Several Alberta Team Trials were held in Edmonton, and times from the 1992 trials stood as track records for more than 10 years.

With facilities unchanged since 1975, the club secured new trailers in 1999 to improve the dressing rooms at Victoria Park. Even so, the site was limited by modern standards and couldn't host significant events.

In 2015 the City of Edmonton opened the Victoria Park Pavilion, a permanent building with enough space for everyone who uses the oval. ESSA leaders Jules Chabot and Jim Kapeluck were instrumental in getting the Pavilion built, ESSA members made significant financial contributions, and ESSA now has a members-only area inside the Pavilion.

The Victoria Park Oval is the most scenic outdoor speed skating track in Canada, and its location in the North Saskatchewan River valley shelters it from bad weather.

### People

**Willem Langenburg** was ESSA's head coach from 1978 to 1992 and Alberta's provincial coach from 1980 to 1985, and helped two skaters become Canadian champions. Willem continues to skate and take part with ESSA.

**Kathy Gregg** was ESSA's long-time program director. Kathy skated at the 1976 Olympics in Innsbruck in the 500 m and 1500 m, and at the 1980 Olympics in Lake Placid in the 500 m, 1000 m and 1500 m. She remembers the 1980 opening ceremonies as especially memorable, coming just after Canadian Ken Taylor had helped American hostages escape Iran.

**Jamie Gregg** represented Canada at the 2010 Olympics in the 500 m (long track), and was ranked 10th overall in the 500 m in the 2009–10 season.

**Jessica Gregg** was a member of Canada's Olympic team in 2010 and 2014, and won silver in the 3000 m short track relay at the 2010 Games in Vancouver.

**Tamara Oudenaarden** was an alternate skater in the 500 m (long track) at the 2010 Vancouver Olympics.

ESSA coach **Gordon Goplen** skated for Canada at the 1988 Olympics in Calgary. The Saskatoon native raced the 5000 m and 10,000 m.

**Cheyenne Goh** became Singapore's first-ever Winter Olympian when she represented that nation in the 1500 m (short track) at the 2018 Olympics in PyeongChang, South Korea.
